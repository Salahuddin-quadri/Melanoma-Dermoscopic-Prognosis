Melanoma remains one of the most aggressive forms of skin cancer, where early diagnosis and accurate prognostic assessment—specifically Breslow thickness—are critical for patient survival. Current clinical workflows often rely on subjective visual inspection or invasive histopathology, while existing automated systems frequently suffer from limited data, severe class imbalance, and a lack of interpretability. To address these challenges, this study presents a comprehensive, multimodal deep learning framework designed for robust non-invasive melanoma prognosis.

Our approach integrates state-of-the-art computer vision with structured clinical data through a novel architecture. We utilize a Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone, pretrained with self-supervised DINO-v3 on domain-specific dermoscopic images, to extract rich visual features without heavy reliance on labeled data. These visual features are fused with clinical metadata (e.g., anatomical site, patient demographics) using a cross-attention mechanism that learns complex modality interactions. To mitigate the inherent class imbalance in medical datasets, the training pipeline incorporates Focal Loss and class-aware augmentation, ensuring the model effectively identifies rare, high-risk cases.

Experimental results demonstrate the system's efficacy, achieving a stable Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of approximately 0.47mm in thickness prediction tasks. Beyond quantitative performance, the framework prioritizes clinical utility by providing explainable outputs via attention maps (Grad-CAM), allowing clinicians to verify the anatomical features driving the model’s predictions. This open-source, reproducible pipeline offers a scalable solution for automated melanoma screening and prognosis, bridging the gap between advanced deep learning research and practical clinical decision support.


Intro:
The rising global incidence of skin cancer has created an urgent need for more accurate and efficient diagnostic tools. Melanoma, while accounting for only a fraction of skin cancers, is responsible for the majority of related deaths, with patient survival heavily dependent on early detection and precise prognostic assessment (e.g., Breslow thickness). Current clinical workflows rely on subjective dermoscopic examination and invasive histopathology, processes that are resource-intensive and prone to inter-observer variability. While deep learning offers a promising alternative, most existing systems are limited by single-modality analysis—ignoring crucial patient metadata—and suffer from "black box" opacity, failing to provide the interpretable rationale required for clinical trust. Additionally, the scarcity of annotated medical datasets and severe class imbalance often hinder the development of robust, generalizable models.

This work addresses these limitations through a novel **multimodal deep learning framework** that synergizes **dermoscopic imaging** with **structured clinical metadata**. Unlike conventional approaches, we employ a **Vision Transformer (ViT)** backbone initialized with **self-supervised DINO-v3** weights. This advanced pretraining strategy enables the model to learn rich, domain-specific visual representations from unlabeled data, significantly reducing reliance on expensive manual annotations. These visual features are then dynamically fused with patient demographics and lesion characteristics using a **cross-attention mechanism**, effectively mimicking the holistic assessment performed by expert dermatologists.

To ensure clinical reliability and practical deployment, our system incorporates **Focal Loss** and **class-aware augmentation** to rigorously address data imbalance, ensuring high sensitivity for high-risk malignant cases. Crucially, the model is designed for transparency, generating **explainable attention maps** that highlight diagnostically significant lesion regions. This dual focus on high-performance prognosis (predicting tumor thickness) and interpretability bridges the gap between technical innovation and clinical utility, offering a scalable, trustworthy tool for modern dermatological practice.
