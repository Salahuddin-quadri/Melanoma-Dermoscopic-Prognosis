
Melanoma remains one of the deadliest skin cancers worldwide, and patient outcomes depend critically on early, accurate diagnosis and appropriate prognostic assessment. Conventional workflows rely on dermoscopic screening followed by histopathological confirmation, but these steps can be time‑consuming and subject to inter‑observer variability. To accelerate diagnostic throughput and reduce delays, automated dermoscopy tools must be accurate, well‑calibrated, and interpretable across heterogeneous imaging conditions.

Recent advances in self‑supervised learning and attention mechanisms enable compact, high‑performance models for dermoscopic image analysis. In this project we focus exclusively on dermoscopy and develop a multitask framework that leverages DINO‑initialized Vision Transformer backbones (when available) together with attention‑augmented convolutional modules. The system is designed to produce two outputs concurrently from dermoscopic images: (i) a binary malignancy prediction and (ii) a Breslow thickness estimate, the latter providing a clinically useful prognostic signal derived from image features.

Key design priorities are clinical relevance, interpretability, and deployability. To mitigate class imbalance common in lesion datasets, training uses focal and weighted BCE losses alongside class‑aware augmentation. Interpretability is provided through attention maps and class activation visualizations that highlight image regions driving model outputs, improving clinician trust. Models are evaluated using discrimination and calibration metrics (AUROC, accuracy, precision, recall, F1) and regression metrics (MAE, RMSE, R²), with uncertainty quantified via bootstrap confidence intervals.

By concentrating on dermoscopic imaging and emphasizing explainability and resource‑aware architectures, the proposed system aims to accelerate clinical decision‑making, support triage in low‑resource environments, and deliver a transparent assistive tool for dermatologists.

1.2	Purpose of the project
The primary objective of this project is to develop a robust, interpretable, and deployable deep learning system for melanoma diagnosis and prognostication that integrates dermoscopic screening and histopathological analysis. Specifically, the system aims to:

- Provide accurate binary classification of skin lesions (melanoma vs. benign) from dermoscopic images.
- Estimate Breslow thickness as a continuous prognostic biomarker to assist staging and treatment planning.
- Improve model calibration and uncertainty quantification to increase clinical trustworthiness.
- Produce visual explanations (attention maps/heatmaps) that localize diagnostically relevant regions and support clinician interpretation.

To accomplish these aims the project leverages attention‑augmented convolutional modules for image localization and a lightweight Vision Transformer (DINO‑initialized where appropriate) for robust feature extraction from high‑resolution inputs. Training strategies include focal and weighted BCE losses, class‑aware augmentation, and a weighted multitask loss to balance classification and regression objectives.

1.2.1	Methodology (Current Approach)
The system design follows a dual‑branch architecture:

- Dermoscopy branch: an attention‑augmented CNN processes dermoscopic images, producing classification probabilities and localized attention maps that highlight salient image regions.
- Histopathology branch: a ViT‑based encoder processes tiled or downsampled WSI patches and contributes high‑resolution morphological features where biopsy data are available.
- Multitask head: a shared projection layer feeds two lightweight heads—one for binary classification (with sigmoid output) and one for regression (thickness estimate).

Model training emphasizes class imbalance mitigation (focal/weighted losses), data augmentation, and calibration techniques; evaluation uses discrimination (AUROC, accuracy, precision, recall, F1), regression (MAE, RMSE, R²), calibration (ECE), and bootstrap confidence intervals for uncertainty.

1.2.2	Project Workflow
The project follows an end‑to‑end experimental pipeline:

- Data acquisition: collect and curate dermoscopic images (ISIC/Kaggle) and public histopathology WSIs.
- Preprocessing: image resizing, normalization, stain normalization (for WSIs), and augmentation.
- Model development: implement attention‑CNN and ViT backbones, dual‑head multitask layer, and training utilities (losses, schedulers, checkpoints).
- Training & validation: train models with stratified splits, early stopping, and checkpointing; evaluate on held‑out cohorts.
- Explainability & visualization: generate attention maps, Grad‑CAM overlays, and calibration plots for clinical review.

1.2.3	Clinical Relevance and Benefits
The integrated pipeline accelerates diagnosis and provides prognostic information in a single pass, reducing time to triage and supporting personalized management plans. Attention maps and calibrated probabilities increase transparency, facilitating clinician acceptance and safer decision support. The architecture is intentionally resource‑aware to enable deployment in constrained clinical settings and telemedicine scenarios.

1.3	Scope of the Project
This project focuses on algorithm development, experimental evaluation, and reproducible workflows. In scope are:

- Model design and training using publicly available dermoscopic and histopathology datasets.
- Implementation of attention mechanisms, multitask heads, and imbalance‑aware training strategies.
- Quantitative evaluation using standard classification, regression, and calibration metrics, plus uncertainty estimation via bootstrapping.
- Visual explainability tools (attention heatmaps, Grad‑CAM) for qualitative clinician assessment.

Out of scope for this work are live clinical deployment, integration with hospital EHR systems, prospective clinical trials, and regulatory approval; these are left as future steps once model validity is established.

1.4	Definitions
•	Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)

•	Dermoscopic Images
Dermoscopic images are magnified, high‑resolution clinical photographs captured with a dermatoscope that reveal subsurface skin patterns and structures useful for non‑invasive melanoma screening.


•	Attention Mechanism
A computational module that weights different parts of the input according to relevance, enabling models to focus on informative regions and improving interpretability.

•	Breslow Thickness
Breslow thickness is a histopathological measurement (in millimeters) of melanoma depth and a key prognostic indicator used in staging and treatment planning.

•	Precision and Recall
Precision (positive predictive value) is the proportion of positive predictions that are correct. Recall (sensitivity) is the proportion of actual positives correctly identified.
